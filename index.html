Study notes · Mistakes, errors, form and use

Mistake (slip): you know the rule and can fix it when you notice it. It happens because you are tired, fast or distracted.

Error: a pattern you repeat because your rule is missing or wrong. You often don't notice it. This course targets your persistent errors.

Error of form: the structure is built incorrectly.
She has went → gone home.

Error of use: the structure is built correctly, but it's the wrong choice for the meaning or context.
I have seen → saw that film last week. (correct present perfect, but last week is finished time)

Course error codes (you will use these all term):

T tense choiceVF verb formSVA subject–verb agreementArt article Prep prepositionWO word orderWF word formSS sentence structure (run-on, comma splice, fragment)

Study notes · The English tense system: the big picture

Every time you use a verb, you make two choices: the time (past, present or future) and the aspect (how you see the action). 3 times × 4 aspects = 12 forms.

AspectBasic structureCore ideaExample
Simpleverb / did / will + verbthe whole action: a fact, habit or complete eventShe works in Lima. She worked there in 2020.
Continuousbe + verb-ingin progress, temporary or unfinished at that momentShe was working when I called.
Perfecthave + past participlelooks back from one time to an earlier time; connects two timesShe has worked here since May.
Perfect continuoushave been + verb-ingduration of an activity up to a point in timeShe had been working for hours.

The time comes from the auxiliary: am/is/are, have/has, do/does = present · was/were, had, did = past · will = future.
